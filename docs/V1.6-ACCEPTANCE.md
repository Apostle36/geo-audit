Implemented a separate action journal, explicit acceptance reviews, conservative
compatible-run comparisons, and optional client-report sections. Run schema
1.4.0 and scoring rubric 1.2.0 are unchanged. Existing audits and the two earlier
demo-site pilots were preserved. The tested 1.5.1 baseline is saved in commit
`5fd9eaf`; implementation continues on `codex/action-tracking-v1-6`.

## Live public business-site check

Used [Plausible's homepage](https://plausible.io/),
[company page](https://plausible.io/about) and
[product explanation](https://plausible.io/simple-web-analytics).
These are public business pages; this was a scoped collection and action-workflow
acceptance check, not a comprehensive GEO audit or a client delivery.

The baseline deliberately used the default unconfigured content condition. Its
actual collector metadata supports one improvement task: configure an explicit
expected-content check. Assigned an owner and a 0.25-hour planning estimate,
marked it in progress, then ready to verify **before** collecting the follow-up.

The follow-up used identical sample/scope/rationale, with `h1`, minimum count
one, a 5000 ms condition timeout and 500 ms initial settling. All six fresh
captures recorded the condition as met with at least one visible element. The
saved DOM files match their embedded evidence; all twelve baseline/follow-up PNG
hashes match the captured screenshot hashes.

Codex explicitly reviewed the six fresh records as an **agent** against the
frozen acceptance check. The passing review made the collection task verified.
The comparison reports **0 new, 0 recurring, 1 resolved and 1 unresolved**. The
unresolved item concerns unmeasured field performance and live AI visibility.
No production website fix, SEO uplift or human approval is asserted.

Without the journal, the same comparison reports zero resolved and two
unresolved. Reformatting a copy of the follow-up likewise removes resolution:
the proof belongs only to the exact reviewed bytes. Repeating resume for both
workflows preserves the run and action-history hashes and creates no new
collection attempts. No live interruption was induced in this acceptance run;
interruption recovery remains covered by the earlier pilot and automated tests.

## Report usefulness and review limits

Exported HTML and a 13-page PDF, both visibly draft. The comparison names the
resolved collection gap, its reviewer type and rationale, the original
acceptance check, unresolved work, and both sets of capture conditions. Action
tracking retains the owner, estimate and status. The documents withhold an
overall score and show the limited assessment scope.

Inspected rendered comparison and tracking pages. The collection-condition
heading stays with its table; the table and action details fit without clipped
text. PDF text extraction retains the acceptance and review details, plus draft
labels. The report exposes readable page labels rather than internal evidence
IDs or snapshot hashes. Review effort was an agent review of six records;
human review time was not measured and no delivery approval was created.
